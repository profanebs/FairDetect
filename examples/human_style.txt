Synthetic example: human-style writing

I started paying attention to the street trees on my walk to the station last winter, mostly because one of them lost a big branch in a storm and nobody came to clear it for three weeks. After that I began noticing how uneven the planting is. The newer blocks have young maples with metal guards, while the older blocks have these huge plane trees that lift the pavement and drop leaves into every drain. The city's inventory says there are about 41,000 street trees, but the map I found online stops updating somewhere in 2019.

What interested me was not the number. It was the difference between the trees the city talks about and the trees people actually use. The big plane trees give real shade in July, and the bus stop on King Street is always crowded because of one of them. The new maples are prettier in photographs and almost useless for shade. I do not know whether the planting plan accounts for that, or whether it is just easier to plant a small tree than to maintain a large one.

I wrote this down because I kept seeing the same pattern in the maintenance reports: the trees that cause problems are the trees that do the most work. That seems like a useful thing to remember before someone decides to replace them all with something tidier.
